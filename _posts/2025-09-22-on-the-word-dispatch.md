*Dispatch* is a good word for travel writing. It implies speed, urgency, the communiqué sent from the field. It also implies a sender and a recipient — someone who went somewhere and someone waiting to hear about it. This dyad is the structural core of travel writing as a genre, more fundamental than any convention of form or style.

The word entered English from Old French *despecher*, to free from impediment. A dispatch is a message freed — extracted from the place where it was produced and sent to where it is needed. There is something physical in it, something that the word *post* or *update* lacks.

I have been thinking about this because I am starting a section of this site for travel writing and I needed a label for it. *Travel* is accurate but flat. *Reports* sounds bureaucratic. *Dispatches* is the one that felt right, though I am aware of the slight self-dramatisation involved.

The genre has always been slightly self-dramatising. That is not a flaw. It is what the form is for.
